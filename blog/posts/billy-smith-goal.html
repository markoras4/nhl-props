Billy Smith didn't shoot the puck. He didn't take aim at an empty net or loft one down the ice to beat the clock. Yet on November 28, 1979, in Denver, he became the first goaltender in NHL history to be credited with a goal, because a puck bounced off his chest and an opponent made a pass to nobody.

Forty-seven years later, goalie goals have stopped being a once-in-a-generation oddity. Igor Shesterkin scored one on October 1. But the first of them belongs to an Islanders goalie with a famous temper, a rebound that went the wrong way, and a rule about who touched the puck last.

Battlin' Billy

Smith came into the league with the Los Angeles Kings, who drafted him in the fifth round in 1970. When the Islanders were stocked through the expansion draft in June 1972, he was the second player they picked. By 1979 he was 28 and known around the league for how he guarded his crease. Forwards who parked too close tended to meet his stick. The nicknames came quickly: "Battlin' Billy", and less kindly, "Hatchet Man".

Nobody was calling the Islanders a dynasty yet. In late November 1979 they were carrying a losing record, and the season that would change everything for them was still finding its feet.

A delayed penalty in Denver

The Islanders were at McNichols Sports Arena to play the Colorado Rockies, a young team with a rookie defenceman named Rob Ramage, who had been the first overall pick in the draft that summer. Early in the third period, at 4:50, the referee raised his arm for a delayed penalty against New York.

On a delayed penalty, the team about to go on the power play can pull its goalie for an extra skater without much risk. The whistle blows as soon as the other side gains control of the puck, so the empty net is only exposed if something very strange happens. Colorado sent goalie Bill McKenzie to the bench and pressed.

Something very strange happened. Ramage's shot hit Smith in the chest protector and bounced into the corner. Ramage went and collected it and made a blind pass back out. There was no one there to take it. The puck carried all the way down the ice and into the empty Colorado net.

“Who was the last guy on the other team to touch the puck? It was me, so I got credit for the goal.”Billy Smith, as quoted by NHL.com

Under the scoring rules, a goal that a team puts into its own net is credited to the last player on the other side to touch the puck. That was Smith, a split second earlier, when the shot came off his chest. In his own words, Ramage "came in and took the shot and it bounced off of me and he picked it up in the corner." The official scorer did the rest.

The Rockies won the game 7–4, so the first goalie goal in NHL history came in a defeat. It barely mattered. That spring the Islanders won the first of four straight Stanley Cups, and Smith won 15 playoff games on the way.

Eight years until someone did it properly

For a long time Smith's goal looked like it would stay a footnote: a scoring rule meeting a bad pass. The first goalie to actually shoot the puck into a net was Ron Hextall of the Philadelphia Flyers, on December 8, 1987. The Bruins pulled Rejean Lemelin late in the game, Hextall stopped the puck and fired it the length of the Spectrum ice at 18:48 of the third period. The Flyers won 5–2.

“Before you guys say anything, I was aiming for that corner.”Ron Hextall, after the game

Hextall did it again in the playoffs on April 11, 1989, against Washington, shorthanded, in an 8–5 win. It is still the only goalie goal ever scored shorthanded.

The list that keeps growing

Counting Smith's, NHL.com lists 21 goals by 18 different goaltenders, two of them in the playoffs. Thirteen were shot into an empty net. Eight were like Smith's: own goals credited to the goalie because he was the last of his team to touch the puck.

Some of them have their own small stories. Damian Rhodes of Ottawa was credited with a goal on January 2, 1999, in a game he also won with a shutout. Evgeni Nabokov scored the first power-play goal by a goalie in 2002. Martin Brodeur has three, one shot and two credited, and his credited goal in 2000 makes him the only goalie with a game-winning goal. Mike Smith beat the clock in 2013 with a tenth of a second left. Alex Nedeljkovic scored for Pittsburgh in January 2025 and added an assist in the same game, a first for a goalie.

For a long time they came a few per decade. Lately they have been coming every season or two.

Goalie goals by decade

NHL regular season and playoffs, shot and credited. The 2020s are not finished and already lead.

Pekka Rinne scored in January 2020. Linus Ullmark followed in 2023, then Tristan Jarry, Filip Gustavsson and Nedeljkovic. On March 1, 2025, more than 45 years after Smith, the Islanders had a goalie credited with a goal again: Ilya Sorokin, when Nashville's Steven Stamkos put the puck into his own empty net. And on October 1 this season, Shesterkin cleared a puck into an empty Tampa Bay net with 2:34 left in a 5–1 Rangers win at Madison Square Garden, the 21st goalie goal in league history.

Why it still matters

Smith went on to a Hall of Fame career. He won the Vezina Trophy in 1982 and the Conn Smythe in 1983, shared the Jennings Trophy that year with Roland Melanson, and retired in 1989 with 88 playoff wins, the most by any goalie at the time. The Islanders retired his No. 31 on February 20, 1993, and he went into the Hall of Fame the same year.

None of that is why he comes up every time a goalie scores. He comes up because he was first, and because of how he did it: not with a clever clearance or a long shot under pressure, but by being in the way of a rebound, a few seconds before somebody else's mistake. It is the most hockey of all firsts: a goal nobody meant to score, credited to the one player on the ice who wasn't trying to.
